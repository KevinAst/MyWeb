M{ bibleLink(`act.12@@Acts chapter 12`) }M presents a difficult moment
in the life of the early church. James is executed by Herod, while
Peter is miraculously delivered from prison by an angel of the
Lord. Both men loved Jesus. Both served Him faithfully. Yet God
allowed one to suffer death while rescuing the other.

Scripture does not try to remove the tension of this moment. Instead,
it reminds us that the Lord’s ways are higher than ours. There are
times when God’s purposes remain beyond our understanding. We
naturally measure circumstances by what seems fair or unfair to us,
but God sees the whole picture while we only see a small part. Faith
often requires trusting the character of God even when we cannot trace
His hand.

The enemy has always tried to convince people that God is not
good. From the Garden of Eden until now, Satan whispers doubts about
the Lord’s character whenever suffering or disappointment enters our
lives. But the cross of Christ settles forever the question of God’s
goodness. A Father who gave His Son to save sinners cannot be accused
of indifference toward His children.

Paul learned this lesson when he pleaded with God to remove his thorn
in the flesh. The Lord did not remove the burden, but He promised
sufficient grace. Sometimes God delivers us from trials, and sometimes
He strengthens us through them. In both cases, He remains faithful.

Acts 12 also reminds us that God will have the final word. Herod
appeared powerful for a moment, but the Lord judged him in the end. No
injustice escapes the eyes of God. One day every wrong will be made
right in His perfect wisdom and justice.

Until then, believers are called to walk by faith, rest in the
goodness of God, and persevere in hope through Jesus Christ.
