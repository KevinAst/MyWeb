Ecclesiastes reminds us that life cannot be understood rightly when
God is removed from the center.

Solomon had experienced what many people spend their lives
pursuing. He possessed wealth, influence, accomplishment, knowledge,
and pleasure. Yet when he examined life merely “under the sun,”
everything seemed like vapor. The Hebrew idea behind vanity describes
something fleeting and difficult to grasp. Life moves quickly,
circumstances change, generations come and go, and much of what we try
to control remains beyond our control.

The problem was not that life had no value. The problem was trying to
find lasting meaning in things that were never designed to provide it.

Ecclesiastes repeatedly exposes the limitations of an earthly
perspective. Possessions cannot satisfy the soul. Accomplishments
cannot stop time. Human wisdom cannot explain every circumstance. Even
recognition eventually fades. When we expect temporary things to give
us permanent meaning, disappointment is inevitable.

God, however, gives us the perspective we cannot find by looking only
at life under the sun. He is eternal when everything around us is
temporary. He is faithful when circumstances are unpredictable. And
through Jesus Christ, we are given something this world cannot
provide: forgiveness, reconciliation with God, and eternal life.

This does not mean believers will understand everything that
happens. Scripture never promises that. Instead, we are called to
trust the One who understands what we cannot. Our past can be placed
in His hands, our future can be entrusted to His care, and our present
can be lived in faithful obedience to Him.

Life is brief, but it is not meaningless when it is lived before God.
