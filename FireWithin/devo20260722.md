Today’s Scripture reveals the great contrast between the fall of
humanity and the redeeming work of Christ.

From the beginning, God created mankind to enjoy fellowship with
Him. Yet Adam’s disobedience brought sin into the world, and with sin
came death. Because Adam stood as the representative of the human
race, every person inherits a sinful nature. The evidence of that
condition is seen in the choices we all make apart from God. Scripture
leaves no room for self-righteousness, for all have sinned and fall
short of God’s glory.

But Romans 5 does not leave us with the tragedy of Adam’s failure. It
points us to Jesus Christ. Where Adam disobeyed, Jesus obeyed the
Father completely. Where Adam brought condemnation, Jesus offers
justification. Where sin brought death, God’s grace now offers eternal
life to all who place their faith in Christ.

Salvation is not something we earn through good works or personal
effort. It is the gracious gift of God, purchased by the sacrifice of
His Son. Christ took upon Himself the judgment our sins deserved so
that we might be declared righteous before God. This is the heart of
the gospel. Our hope rests not in our ability to overcome sin, but in
Christ’s finished work on the cross.

As believers, we are called to live each day in gratitude for what
Christ has accomplished. His grace not only forgives our past, but
also enables us to walk in faithful obedience as those who have been
made new.

The cross reminds us that our greatest problem has already been
answered by God’s greatest provision in Jesus Christ.
