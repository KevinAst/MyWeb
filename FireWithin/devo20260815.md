Today’s Scripture reminds us that God’s truth does not change when the
world around us does.

Jeremiah ministered during a time of serious spiritual decline. God’s
people had wandered from His Word, yet the Lord graciously called them
to stop, consider their ways, and return to the path He had already
revealed. The problem was not that they lacked direction. God had
spoken clearly. The question was whether they would listen and obey.

The "ancient paths" were not an invitation to nostalgia. They pointed
God’s people back to His established truth. Their safety was not found
in following whatever seemed acceptable in their generation, but in
walking according to what God had said.

That same principle remains important for believers today. Scripture
must shape the way we understand every area of life. We are not called
to adjust God’s Word to fit our preferences, fears, loyalties, or the
prevailing opinions around us. Instead, we bring our thinking under
the authority of Scripture.

This requires humility. Sometimes God’s Word confronts assumptions we
have carried for years. Sometimes obedience places us at odds with
people we respect. But faithfulness to Christ means that our first
concern is pleasing Him.

Jesus perfectly walked in obedience to the Father, and through His
death and resurrection He has provided salvation for sinners who could
never make themselves righteous. Following God’s way, therefore, is
not an attempt to earn His favor. It is the response of those who have
received grace and now desire to live under Christ’s lordship.

When you find yourself at a crossroads, do not begin by asking what is
popular, convenient, or personally comfortable. Begin with what God
has said, and ask Him for the grace to walk faithfully in it.
