Today’s Scripture reminds us that the Christian life begins and
continues by the grace of God.

Grace is often understood simply as God’s undeserved favor toward
sinners, and rightly so. We cannot earn salvation through good works,
religious effort, or personal righteousness. God saves us because He
is gracious, and salvation is received through faith in Jesus Christ.

But in M{ bibleLink(`tit.2@@Titus 2`) }M, Paul explains that grace
does more than bring salvation. Grace also teaches us how to live. The
same grace that brings us to Christ begins changing the way we think,
speak, and conduct ourselves. It teaches us to turn away from
ungodliness and to live with self-control, righteousness, and devotion
to God.

This means obedience is not an attempt to earn God’s acceptance. It is
the proper response of someone who has already received His
mercy. When we understand what Christ has done for us, our hearts
should increasingly desire what pleases Him.

Jesus gave Himself to redeem us from sin and to make us His own. His
blood paid the price we could never pay. Therefore, Christian living
is not merely a matter of following rules. It is the life of a
redeemed person learning to walk according to the truth of God’s Word.

Sound doctrine and godly living belong together. What we believe about
God should shape how we live before Him. Grace never gives us
permission to remain comfortable with sin. Instead, grace patiently
trains us to belong more fully to Christ in our daily conduct.

Consider today whether your understanding of God’s grace is
producing greater obedience, self-control, and gratitude toward
Christ.
