These words remind us that God’s faithfulness remains sure even when
the consequences of sin are painfully clear.

Lamentations was written in the shadow of Jerusalem’s destruction. The
city had suffered greatly because the people repeatedly rejected God’s
warnings and continued in rebellion. Jeremiah did not minimize their
sin or its consequences. He grieved over what disobedience had
produced.

Yet in the very center of that sorrow, Jeremiah turned his attention
to the character of God.

God had not changed. His mercy had not disappeared. His compassion had
not failed. Even when His people experienced discipline, He remained
faithful to His nature and His promises.

This is an important truth for every believer. Sin is serious, and
Scripture never encourages us to treat it lightly. Unrepentant sin
brings guilt, sorrow, spiritual unrest, and painful consequences. But
our failures are never greater than the mercy God provides to those
who turn to Him in repentance and faith.

The hope of Lamentations ultimately points us toward Christ. At the
cross, God did not ignore our sin. Jesus bore its judgment so that
forgiveness could be offered freely to all who trust in Him. We do not
receive mercy because we deserve another opportunity. We receive mercy
because God is compassionate, faithful, and gracious.

Perhaps yesterday included failure, regret, or disobedience. The
answer is not to hide from God or carry guilt as though Christ’s
sacrifice were insufficient. Confess your sin, turn from it, and
receive the cleansing He promises.

God’s mercies being new every morning does not make sin less
serious. It makes His grace all the more precious.

When your failures seem great, remember that the faithfulness of God
is greater still.
