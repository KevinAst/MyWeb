The opening chapter of Romans reminds us that the gospel is not merely
religious information. It is the good news of what God has done
through Jesus Christ to save sinners. Before we can appreciate that
good news, however, we must understand why we need it. Scripture
teaches that God is both loving and holy. Because He is holy, He
cannot ignore sin. Because He is loving, He provided a Savior.

Paul writes that the gospel is the power of God for
salvation. Salvation is not achieved through human effort, personal
merit, or religious performance. It comes through faith in Jesus
Christ. This truth stands at the heart of Romans. God extends His
grace, His undeserved favor, to people who could never earn His
acceptance on their own.

At the same time, Romans teaches that no one is without accountability
before God. Creation itself bears witness to His existence, power, and
wisdom. Every sunrise, every star in the heavens, and every detail of
life points beyond itself to the Creator. Yet the human heart is often
tempted to suppress that truth and live independently of God. When we
do, we exchange the worship of the Creator for lesser things that can
never satisfy.

The beauty of the gospel is that God does not leave us in our
guilt. He meets us with grace. Christ took upon Himself the judgment
our sins deserved so that all who trust in Him might be forgiven and
reconciled to God. The Christian life begins and continues by faith,
resting not in what we have done for God, but in what God has done for
us through His Son.
