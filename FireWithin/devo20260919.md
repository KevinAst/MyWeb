Rest is something everyone wants, yet surprisingly few possess. You
can take a day off, go on vacation, turn off your phone, and still
have a heart that refuses to be quiet. Physical rest is important, but
Hebrews points us toward something far greater—the rest found in Jesus
Christ.

Israel learned this lesson the hard way. God delivered His people from
slavery in Egypt and promised to bring them into a land of rest. Yet
an entire generation failed to enjoy what God had prepared because
unbelief produced disobedience. They had heard God’s promise, but they
did not respond to it with faith.

There is a warning here for us. It is possible to hear God’s Word,
know His promises, and still live as though everything depends upon
us. Unbelief keeps striving, worrying, controlling, and carrying
burdens God never intended us to carry alone.

Jesus offers something better.

He said, “Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I
will give you rest” (M{ bibleLink(`mat.11.28@@Matthew 11:28@@NKJV`) }M).
Notice where that rest is found. Jesus did not point to a place, a
schedule, or better circumstances. He said, “Come to Me.”

The deepest rest of the soul is not the absence of trouble; it is the
presence of Christ. When you belong to Him, you can face an uncertain
tomorrow knowing that your life is held securely in His hands. Your
sins have been forgiven. Your eternity is settled. Your burdens can be
brought to Him. You no longer have to earn what Jesus has already
accomplished for you.

God also knows our physical limitations. He established a pattern of
work and rest because we were never designed to run endlessly. There
are times when the most obedient thing you can do is stop, be still,
and remember that God remains God even when you are not working.

But even the best night’s sleep cannot satisfy a restless soul. Only
Christ can do that.

So what are you carrying today? Fear about tomorrow? Regret from
yesterday? Pressure to keep everything together? Bring it to
Jesus. Believe His Word and refuse to let unbelief rob you of what He
has promised.
