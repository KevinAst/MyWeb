This passage reveals God’s gracious invitation to people who could not
cleanse themselves.

Isaiah spoke to Judah during a time of serious spiritual
rebellion. The people continued their religious practices, but their
hearts were far from God. They had become comfortable with sin,
neglected the works of the Lord, and exchanged God’s standard of
righteousness for their own. Their outward religion could not conceal
their inward condition before Him.

Yet M{ bibleLink(`isa.1@@Isaiah 1`) }M does not leave them without
hope. In the midst of God’s rebuke comes a remarkable invitation:
“Come now.” God exposes sin truthfully, but He also calls sinners to
Himself graciously. The One against whom they had rebelled was the
very One offering cleansing.

Scarlet and crimson picture the deep stain of sin. We cannot remove
that stain through religious activity, good intentions, or greater
effort. Scripture teaches that our deepest problem requires a work of
God that we cannot accomplish for ourselves.

This is where the gospel becomes precious. Jesus Christ came to do
what sinners could never do. He bore our sins on the cross and shed
His blood so that everyone who comes to Him in faith can receive
forgiveness and cleansing. Grace does not pretend that sin is
insignificant. Grace provides the remedy for sin through Christ.

Therefore, when God reveals sin in our lives, our response should not
be to hide it, excuse it, or attempt to compensate for it with
religious activity. We are called to come to Him in repentance and
faith, trusting His provision rather than our own efforts.

The stain of sin is beyond our ability to remove, but it is never
beyond the cleansing grace of God in Christ.
