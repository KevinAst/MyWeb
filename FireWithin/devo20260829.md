Today’s Scripture reminds us that our greatest spiritual need begins
with recognizing the true condition of the human heart.

It is easy to measure ourselves against other people and conclude that
we are doing reasonably well. We may look at someone else’s failures
and think, “I have never done that.” But Scripture does not measure
righteousness by comparing one sinner with another. God measures us
according to His own holiness, and by that standard, none of us is
righteous.

Sin is not merely a collection of outward actions. Jesus taught that
sinful behavior flows from within the heart. Anger, lust, pride,
greed, deceit, and bitterness reveal something deeper than isolated
mistakes. They reveal our need for a Savior.

This is why minimizing sin is spiritually dangerous. If we convince
ourselves that the wound is small, we will never understand why we
need the Great Physician. God’s Word speaks truthfully about our
condition, not to drive us into despair, but to lead us to the One who
can forgive and restore us.

The good news of the gospel is not that we can become righteous
through greater effort. Our hope is Jesus Christ. He lived the
righteous life we could never live, bore our sin on the cross, and
offers forgiveness to everyone who comes to Him in faith.

The woman who came to Jesus in brokenness understood something the
self-confident often miss. She knew she needed mercy. She did not
defend herself or compare herself with others. She came to Christ, and
He forgave her.

Grace becomes precious when we understand what we have been
forgiven. The proper response is not shameful hiding, but humble faith
in the Savior who receives sinners.

Remember today that seeing the seriousness of sin should always lead
us to see more clearly the sufficiency of Christ.
