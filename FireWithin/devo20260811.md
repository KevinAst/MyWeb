This passage reminds us that discerning God’s will is closely
connected to a life being transformed by Him.

We often want God to give us direction concerning important
decisions. We ask where we should go, what we should do, or which path
we should choose. Yet Romans 12 teaches us that discernment begins
before the decision. It begins with surrender.

Paul first calls believers to present themselves to God as living
sacrifices. Then he tells us not to be conformed to the thinking of
this world, but to be transformed through the renewing of our
minds. Only then does he speak about discerning the good, acceptable,
and perfect will of God. God is concerned not only with the direction
we take, but also with the kind of people we are becoming as we follow
Him.

A renewed mind comes through the truth of Scripture. As God’s Word
dwells richly within us, we increasingly learn what pleases Him. The
Bible may not name every particular decision we face, but it reveals
God’s character, commands, priorities, and wisdom. These truths shape
our judgment so that we can approach decisions with a mind
increasingly governed by Christ.

This also means we should not expect God’s guidance while knowingly
resisting what He has already revealed. His unknown will should never
become an excuse for neglecting His known will. When Scripture has
spoken clearly, obedience is not something to postpone while we wait
for further direction.

And when we sincerely seek the Lord yet remain uncertain, we can
remember His mercy. Our confidence is not in our ability to make every
decision perfectly, but in the faithfulness of the God who leads His
children.

Before asking God to show you the next step, consider whether your
present steps are already being shaped by His Word.
