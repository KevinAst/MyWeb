This passage reveals that true spiritual restoration begins with what
God does within us.

Ezekiel spoke to a people who had experienced the consequences of
rebellion against God. Israel had profaned His name through idolatry
and disobedience, and judgment had followed. Yet God declared that He
would gather, cleanse, and restore His people. Their restoration would
not be based on their worthiness, but on the faithfulness and holiness
of God.

At the center of this promise is something deeper than returning to a
land or rebuilding ruined places. God promised to give His people a
new heart. Their greatest problem was not merely their
circumstances. It was the condition of their hearts.

That same truth applies to us. We may try to change our behavior,
improve our circumstances, or correct outward failures, but only God
can transform the heart. Sin hardens us toward Him, and no amount of
human effort can produce the spiritual life we need.

This is why the grace of God is so precious. Through Jesus Christ, God
does for sinners what they cannot do for themselves. He forgives,
cleanses, and gives new life to those who come to Christ by faith. The
Christian life is not an attempt to make an old heart behave
better. It is the life that flows from God’s gracious work within us
through His Spirit.

And because salvation begins with God’s grace, our continued walk with
Him must depend upon that same grace. We obey not to earn His favor,
but because He has mercifully made us His own.

Consider today whether you are depending upon outward effort or upon
the transforming work of God within your heart.
