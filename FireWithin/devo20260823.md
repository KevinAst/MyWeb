These words call us to consider whether our outward profession of
faith reflects the true condition of our hearts.

When Jesus confronted the religious leaders, He exposed something more
serious than improper religious practice. They were careful about
outward observance, but their hearts were distant from God. Human
traditions had become more important to them than genuine obedience to
the Lord. Their problem was hypocrisy, an inconsistency between what
they professed and what was actually true within them.

This warning is not merely for the Pharisees. Every believer should
allow Scripture to examine the heart. It is possible to know Christian
language, attend worship, serve faithfully, and still permit areas of
life to remain surrendered more to habit, reputation, or self-interest
than to Christ. God is not satisfied with outward conformity while the
heart remains distant from Him.

Yet the answer to hypocrisy is not pretending to be better. It is
honest repentance. Peter himself demonstrated painful inconsistency
when fear caused him to withdraw from Gentile believers, despite
having learned that God shows no partiality. His failure reminds us
that even sincere believers can stumble into hypocrisy. But failure
does not place us beyond God’s grace. Christ calls us to confess our
sin, receive His forgiveness, and return to faithful obedience.

We should therefore examine ourselves before becoming occupied with
the faults of others. Jesus teaches us to address the plank in our own
eye before attempting to remove the speck from another’s. This
produces humility rather than self-righteousness and allows correction
to come from love rather than pride.

God desires more than words from His people. He desires hearts wholly
devoted to Him, lives in which private conduct increasingly agrees
with public confession.

Ask the Lord to reveal where your words and your walk do not agree,
and surrender those places to Him with an undivided heart.
