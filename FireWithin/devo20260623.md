Today's Scripture reminds us that God's assessment of the human heart
differs greatly from the world's assessment.

Scripture teaches that humanity's greatest problem is not a lack of
improvement, but the reality of sin. The world often assures us that
people are basically good and simply need encouragement or
refinement. Yet Romans presents a different picture. Every person,
regardless of background, morality, or religious knowledge, stands
guilty before a holy God. Paul makes it clear that whether one lives
openly in unrighteousness, trusts in personal goodness, or takes pride
in religious privilege, all have fallen short of God's standard.

This truth humbles us. It removes every basis for self-righteousness
and comparison. We cannot measure ourselves against other people and
conclude that we are acceptable before God. The Lord Himself is the
standard, and His holiness exposes the true condition of our hearts.

Yet God has not left humanity without witness. He has revealed Himself
through creation and has placed within each person a conscience that
testifies to right and wrong. Even so, our conscience cannot save
us. Like a mirror, it reveals our need but cannot cleanse us. The law
of God likewise exposes sin but cannot remove it. Its purpose is to
lead us to recognize our need for a Savior.

The good news of the gospel is that what we could never accomplish
through our own efforts, God has accomplished through Jesus
Christ. Salvation is not earned by human goodness. It is received by
grace through faith in Christ, who bore our sin and satisfied God's
righteous judgment on our behalf.

Remember throughout this day that humility before God is not a place
of despair, but the doorway to grace.
