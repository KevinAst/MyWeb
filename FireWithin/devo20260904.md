This passage reveals that God’s warnings are expressions of His
compassion.

The closing chapters of 2 Chronicles record a sobering period in
Judah’s history. Again and again, the people turned from the
Lord. Their leaders hardened their hearts, ignored His Word, and
resisted the prophets God sent to them. Yet before judgment came, God
repeatedly warned His people.

That detail matters. God was not looking for an opportunity to destroy
them. He was calling them to return.

Second Chronicles 36 tells us that He sent His messengers because He
had compassion on His people. His correction was an invitation to
repentance. His warnings were evidence of patience. But the people
continued to mock His messengers and despise His Word until their
persistent rebellion eventually brought the consequences God had long
warned about.

There is an important lesson for us. When Scripture exposes something
in our lives that needs to change, the right response is humility. We
should not harden our hearts, defend our sin, or postpone
obedience. God’s Word is given for our good, and His correction is one
of the ways He draws us back into faithful fellowship with Him.

The good news of the gospel is that Christ has made the way for
sinners to return to God. Jesus bore the judgment our sins deserve so
that all who trust in Him can receive forgiveness and
grace. Repentance, therefore, is not an attempt to earn God’s
favor. It is the proper response of a heart that has heard His Word
and trusts His mercy.

Do not mistake God’s patience for permission to remain unchanged. When
He speaks through His Word, receive His correction with humility and
return to Him in faith.
