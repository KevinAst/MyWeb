Paul understood that following Christ would not lead to an easy
life. As he traveled from city to city preaching the gospel, he
encountered opposition, rejection, and suffering. In Lystra, after God
healed a man who had never walked, the crowd first praised Paul and
Barnabas as gods, then quickly turned against them and stoned Paul
nearly to death. Yet after recovering, Paul returned to strengthen the
believers and encourage them to remain faithful.

What gave Paul such endurance was not confidence in himself, but
confidence in the Lord. He knew that hardship is not evidence that God
has abandoned His people. Rather, trials often become the means by
which God deepens faith, shapes character, and teaches dependence upon
Him. The Christian life is not free from suffering, but it is never
without purpose.

Scripture repeatedly reminds us that God uses adversity for our
spiritual growth. Trials teach patience because they force us to wait
upon the Lord. They produce perseverance because faith must continue
even when circumstances are difficult. They give perspective because
suffering loosens our grip on temporary things and fixes our eyes on
eternity. Most importantly, hardships remind us of the presence of
God. In seasons of ease we can become self-reliant, but in weakness we
often draw near to the Lord in deeper ways.

Jesus never promised His followers a trouble-free life. He promised
His presence in the midst of it. The same Savior who suffered for us
now walks with us through every burden we face. Nothing that comes
into the believer’s life is outside His sovereign care.
