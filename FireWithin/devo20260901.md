M{ bibleLink(`isa.35@@Isaiah 35`) }M reminds us that God does not
abandon His people in barren places.

The chapter looks beyond present trouble to a day when God will
restore what has been devastated. The wilderness will blossom, the
weak will be strengthened, and the redeemed will enter Zion with
joy. Ultimately, this points to the righteous reign of the Lord and
the restoration that comes through Him.

Yet this passage also teaches us something important about walking
with God through seasons of weakness. Scripture never promises that
believers will avoid sorrow, hardship, or spiritual weariness. There
are times when our hands become weak, our knees feel unsteady, and
fear presses upon the heart. God does not tell us to pretend these
things are not real. Instead, He directs our attention to Himself.

The hope of M{ bibleLink(`isa.35@@Isaiah 35`) }M rests in what God
will do. He comes to save. He strengthens the weak. He brings water
into the wilderness and streams into the desert. The barren place does
not have the final word because God is present with His people.

Sometimes the Lord changes our circumstances. At other times, He gives
sufficient grace to sustain us within them. Paul learned this when the
Lord told him that divine power is made perfect in weakness
(M{ bibleLink(`2co.12.9-10@@2 Corinthians 12:9-10`) }M). Our
confidence, therefore, is not that every difficulty will quickly
disappear, but that Christ remains faithful when our own strength is
gone.

The desert can also become a place where we see God’s sufficiency more
clearly. What we cannot produce for ourselves, He provides by
grace. The God who saves us through Christ is also the God who keeps
us as we walk with Christ.

When your strength is small, remember that your security rests not in
your ability to endure, but in the faithfulness of the God who holds
you.
