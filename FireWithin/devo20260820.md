Today’s Scripture reminds us that spiritual formation begins with the
truth God has revealed in His Word.

Jesus prayed that His followers would be sanctified by
truth. Sanctification is the continuing work of God in believers,
shaping our thinking, desires, and conduct so that we increasingly
reflect Christ. And Jesus identified the standard of that truth
clearly: God’s Word.

That matters because many voices compete for our attention and
influence. The material provided emphasizes the importance of biblical
teaching, especially in shaping families and the next generation. But
Scripture reminds us that our confidence is not ultimately in
programs, personalities, traditions, or human wisdom. The church must
continually return to what God has spoken.

This responsibility begins personally. Before we can faithfully teach
God’s Word to others, we must allow it to govern our own hearts. We
are called not merely to defend biblical truth, but to submit to
it. The Bible corrects our thinking, exposes sin, renews our minds,
and teaches us how to walk in obedience to Christ.

This also shapes the way believers influence others. Children
especially need more than moral instruction. They need to know the God
who created them, the reality of sin, and the grace offered through
Jesus Christ. Christian discipleship is not simply teaching people to
behave better. It is pointing them to Christ and teaching them to
understand life according to His Word.

When Scripture remains central, we do not have to invent truth or
reshape it according to changing opinions. We receive it humbly, live
under its authority, and faithfully pass it on.

The question is not merely whether we believe the Bible is true,
but whether we are allowing its truth to shape the way we think, live,
and teach others.
