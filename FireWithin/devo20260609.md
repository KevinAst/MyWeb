Paul’s first recorded sermon in Acts 13 came after years of quiet
preparation before the Lord. When he finally stood in the synagogue to
speak, his message centered on one great truth: salvation comes
through Jesus Christ, not through human effort.

The Jews listening to Paul were devoted to the Law of Moses. They
believed righteousness came through obedience and sacrifice. Yet Paul
explained that the law could never fully save anyone because the
deeper problem was the human heart. The law reveals God’s holy
standard, exposes our sinful condition, and shows us our need for a
Savior, but only Jesus can forgive sin and make a person righteous
before God.

This is the beauty of grace. Grace is God’s undeserved kindness toward
sinners. We cannot earn it through moral effort, religious activity,
or personal discipline. Salvation is a gift given through faith in
Christ alone. The cross reminds us that Jesus accomplished what we
never could. He bore our sin, satisfied the justice of God, and rose
again so that all who believe in Him might be justified before the
Father.

The Christian life does not begin by grace and continue by
self-effort. We grow the same way we were saved, by depending daily on
the Lord. As we read God’s Word, the Spirit reveals areas that need
correction, not to condemn us, but to draw us nearer to Christ. The
Lord does not expose our weakness to destroy us. He does so in mercy,
so we will rest more fully in His grace.
