This passage reminds us that sincerity alone cannot protect us from
spiritual deception.

Paul was deeply concerned for the Corinthian believers because false
teachers were presenting a distorted understanding of Jesus. Their
message may have sounded spiritual and persuasive, but it was not
faithful to the Christ revealed through the apostles. Paul understood
that deception often succeeds not by openly rejecting truth, but by
subtly changing it.

That warning remains important for every believer. We do not have the
freedom to redefine Jesus according to personal preference, religious
tradition, or cultural expectations. We must know Him as Scripture
reveals Him.

Jesus is the Christ, the Son of the living God. He is God who came in
the flesh, lived without sin, died as the atoning sacrifice for our
sins, rose bodily from the grave, ascended to heaven, and will come
again. Salvation is not found merely in admiring Jesus as a teacher or
acknowledging Him as a religious figure. We are saved by grace through
faith in the true Christ and His finished work.

This is why knowing Scripture matters. Counterfeit teaching becomes
more difficult to recognize when our understanding of the genuine
truth is shallow. Satan is a deceiver, and Scripture warns that
deception can appear convincing and even religious. Therefore,
believers must continually bring every teaching back to God’s Word.

The answer to deception is not fear, but faithfulness. Remain in
Scripture. Know the gospel. Fix your attention on Christ. The more
clearly we know the Jesus revealed in God’s Word, the better prepared
we are to recognize teaching that distorts His identity or His saving
work.

Let Scripture, rather than culture or human opinion, determine who
Jesus is.
