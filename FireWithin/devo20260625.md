Today's Scripture reminds us that our right standing with God is
founded entirely upon what Christ has done for us.

The opening chapters of Romans present a sobering reality. Every
person stands guilty before a holy God because all have sinned and
fallen short of His perfect standard. Scripture leaves no room for
self-righteousness or confidence in our own goodness. We may compare
ourselves with others and believe that we measure up reasonably well,
but God's standard is not other people. His standard is His own
holiness.

Yet Romans does not leave us in despair. After revealing humanity's
need, Paul turns to the glorious good news of the gospel. God, in His
love and mercy, provided what we could never provide for
ourselves. Jesus Christ, the sinless Son of God, took upon Himself the
judgment our sins deserved. Through His sacrificial death and
resurrection, He satisfied the righteous demands of God's justice and
opened the way for sinners to be reconciled to God.

This salvation is received by faith, not earned through religious
effort or good works. Abraham himself was declared righteous because
he believed God, long before the law was given. In the same way, we
are justified when we place our trust in Christ alone. Good works have
their proper place in the Christian life, but they are the fruit of
salvation, not the basis of it. We obey God because we have been
saved, not in order to be saved.

The gospel calls us to rest in Christ's finished work. We need not
strive to earn God's favor, for His grace is freely given to all who
believe.

Consider today whether your confidence rests in your own efforts or in
the sufficiency of Jesus Christ.
