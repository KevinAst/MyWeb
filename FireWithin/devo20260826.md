Today’s Scripture reminds us that every part of life is meant to point
beyond ourselves to the greatness of God.

M{ bibleLink(`isa.39@@Isaiah 39`) }M gives us a sobering picture of
King Hezekiah after God had graciously healed him. When
representatives from Babylon came to visit, Hezekiah showed them his
treasures, his storehouses, and his armory. He had an opportunity to
speak of the Lord who had restored his life, but instead the attention
settled on what he possessed.

M{ bibleLink(`isa.40@@Isaiah 40`) }M provides the needed
contrast. Human glory is compared to grass and flowers that quickly
wither and fall, while the glory and Word of God endure
forever. Whatever strength, influence, possessions, ability, or
accomplishment we may have is temporary. God alone is eternal,
incomparable, and worthy of praise.

The danger comes when we begin to treat God’s gifts as though they
originated with us. Scripture does not forbid us from working
diligently or recognizing what has been accomplished. It calls us to
remember the true source of every good thing. Our abilities,
opportunities, resources, and even the breath in our lungs are held by
the gracious hand of God.

Jesus reveals God’s glory most clearly. He came not seeking the praise
of men, but doing the will of the Father. Through His death and
resurrection, Christ accomplished what no human achievement could ever
accomplish: redemption for sinners and reconciliation with God.

Therefore, the believer’s life should increasingly become a testimony
that directs attention to the Lord. Our work, relationships,
possessions, words, and service are not ultimately platforms for
self-exaltation. They are opportunities to honor the One who has given
us everything we possess.

Consider today whether the blessings entrusted to you are drawing
attention to yourself or directing praise to the God who gave them.
