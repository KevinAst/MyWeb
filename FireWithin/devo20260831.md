Today’s Scripture reveals a remarkable truth about the Christian life:
Christ does not merely save us from a distance, He dwells within those
who belong to Him.

Paul describes this truth as a mystery that had been hidden for
generations but was now revealed. In Scripture, a mystery is not
something impossible to understand. It is a truth once concealed in
God’s purposes and later made known by His revelation. And the truth
Paul announces is this: Christ in you, the hope of glory.

The Christian life is therefore more than adopting biblical principles
or trying to imitate the example of Jesus through human
determination. When a person trusts in Christ, the Holy Spirit takes
up residence within that believer. We belong to Christ, and His
presence changes the very foundation from which we live.

This also explains why Christian growth cannot be accomplished merely
through willpower. Paul later speaks of laboring with the energy God
powerfully worked within him _(M{ bibleLink(`col.1.29@@Colossians 1:29`) }M)_.

Paul worked diligently, but he
understood the source of his strength. His ministry was not sustained
by natural ability alone. God was at work within him.

The same principle applies to us. We are called to obey, serve, resist
sin, pursue holiness, and remain faithful. Yet we do these things
depending upon God’s power rather than trusting our own
strength. Biblical dependence is not passivity. It is faithful
obedience that recognizes our sufficiency comes from Christ.

And because Christ is in us, we have the hope of glory. Our hope is
not based upon our ability to hold everything together. It rests upon
the One who has saved us, indwells us, and will ultimately bring His
people into His presence.

The Christian’s confidence is not found in the strength of self, but
in the faithful presence of Christ within.
