Today’s Scripture reminds us that the peace Jesus offers is found in a
restored relationship with God.

As Jesus approached Jerusalem, He did not rejoice over the crowds or
the praise they offered. Instead, He wept because He saw hearts that
were rejecting the very One who had come to save them. His sorrow
revealed the compassion of God for people who were separated from Him
by sin. Christ did not come merely to improve lives or ease earthly
burdens. He came to reconcile sinners to a holy God through His
sacrifice on the cross.

The suffering Jesus endured was not an accident of history but the
fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan. He willingly bore the punishment
that we deserved so that forgiveness could be freely offered to all
who place their faith in Him. The temporary sacrifices of the Old
Testament pointed forward to the perfect sacrifice of the Lamb of God,
whose death was sufficient once for all. Because Christ finished the
work of redemption, we do not earn God’s favor through good works. We
receive His grace by trusting in the finished work of Jesus Christ.

The peace that Christ gives is different from the peace the world
promises. Circumstances may change, trials may come, and sorrow may
remain for a season, yet the believer can rest in the unchanging
certainty that God has made peace with us through His Son. That peace
steadies the heart because it is rooted in God’s faithfulness rather
than our circumstances.

May your heart rest today in the truth that the Savior who wept
over sinners also willingly gave His life so that all who believe in
Him might know the lasting peace of God.
