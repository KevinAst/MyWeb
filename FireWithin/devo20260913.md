Two mountains stand before us in Hebrews 12, and the difference
between them could not be greater.

Mount Sinai shook with thunder, fire, smoke, and the terrifying voice
of God. Boundaries were established, and the people were warned not to
draw near. Even Moses trembled. Sinai displayed the holiness of God
and the demands of His law, leaving sinful man with no doubt that God
is righteous and sin is serious.


But fear produced by outward circumstances could not transform the
human heart. Before long, the same people who trembled at Sinai were
worshiping idols. And therein lies an important truth: **_external
fear cannot accomplish what only an internally transformed heart can
do._**

Then the writer of Hebrews turns our attention to another mountain -
Mount Zion. Here we see Jesus, the Mediator of a new covenant, and His
blood speaking “better things.” Abel’s blood cried out for justice
after Cain murdered him. But the blood of Jesus announces something
far greater for the sinner who believes: mercy, forgiveness, and
grace.

This is the wonder of the Gospel. God did not ignore His justice in
order to forgive you. Jesus satisfied that justice at the cross. The
judgment our sins deserved fell upon Christ, and everyone who places
their faith in Him receives a righteousness they could never earn.

Does that mean grace gives us permission to live carelessly?
Absolutely not! Hebrews immediately reminds us that “our God is a
consuming fire” (M{ bibleLink(`heb.12.29@@Hebrews 12:29@@NKJV`) }M).
Grace does not make God less holy. Rather, understanding what Jesus
endured for us should produce deep gratitude, reverence, and a desire
to obey Him.

You have not been invited to stand trembling at a distance, hoping
your efforts will somehow make you acceptable to God. In Christ, you
are invited to draw near. Your confidence is not in your goodness but
in His finished work.

**_Believer, never treat grace casually._** Worship the Lord with
reverence and gratitude. Let the mercy you have received transform how
you live today. And when your failures tempt you to retreat from God,
remember where you have come—to Jesus and to a kingdom that cannot be
shaken.
