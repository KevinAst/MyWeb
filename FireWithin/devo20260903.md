This passage reminds us that obedience to God is not always
complicated, but it often requires us to surrender our own
expectations.

Naaman was a powerful and respected military commander, yet there was
one problem he could not solve: his leprosy. When he came seeking
healing, he expected the prophet Elisha to respond in a certain
way. Instead, Elisha simply instructed him to wash seven times in the
Jordan River.

Naaman became angry because God’s instruction did not match his
expectation. The command seemed too ordinary. He had imagined
something more impressive, but God required something simple: humble
obedience.

That is often where the struggle lies for us as well. We may be
willing to do something difficult for God while resisting something
simple He has clearly said. 

- Forgive
- Pray
- Tell the truth
- Wait patiently
- Serve quietly
- Turn away from sin
- Trust His Word

The difficulty is not always understanding what God has said.
Sometimes the difficulty is surrendering our desire to do things our
own way.

Naaman’s servants wisely encouraged him to obey. When he finally
submitted to God’s instruction, he was healed. Yet the greater result
was not merely restored skin. Naaman came to recognize the true
God. His physical need became the means through which God brought him
to a deeper knowledge of Himself.

God is not obligated to work according to our expectations. His wisdom
is greater than ours, and His ways are always right. Our
responsibility is not to redesign His instructions but to trust Him
enough to obey them.

Simple obedience can reveal whether we truly trust the One who has
spoken.
