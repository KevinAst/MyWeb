When God looks at our giving, He sees something we cannot see—He sees
the heart. We tend to notice amounts. God notices attitudes. We may be
impressed by the size of a gift, but the Lord weighs the motive behind
it.

Paul pointed the Corinthians to believers who understood this
truth. Though they were experiencing deep poverty themselves, they
willingly gave to help suffering Christians. But something happened
before their generosity ever reached another person: “_They first gave
themselves to the Lord_”
(M{ bibleLink(`2co.8.5@@2 Corinthians 8:5@@NKJV`) }M).

There is the secret to biblical generosity.

Giving begins with surrender.

God is not primarily interested in getting something from your
hand. He wants your heart. Once your life belongs completely to Jesus
Christ, everything else begins to fall into its proper place—your
time, your abilities, your possessions, and yes, your money.

This is why Paul describes giving as a grace rather than a grudging
obligation. The Christian does not give in order to manipulate God
into providing something bigger in return. Nor should we give because
someone has pressured us. We give because God has already been
overwhelmingly generous toward us.

Think about the Gospel. “_For God so loved the world that He gave His
only begotten Son_” (M{ bibleLink(`jhn.3.16@@John 3:16@@NKJV`) }M).

The greatest act of generosity in history came from the heart of
God. Jesus Christ willingly gave Himself upon the cross so that
sinners could be forgiven and receive everlasting life.

When that truth grips your heart, generosity changes from something
you have to do into something you get to do.

Perhaps you can give much. Perhaps you can give very little. The
amount is not the first issue before the Lord. Ask instead, “What is
happening in my heart?” Is there gratitude? Trust? Joy? A willingness
to honor Christ with what He has placed in your hands?

And remember, God is no debtor. The One who supplies the seed also
promises to provide what His children need so that they can continue
in every good work. We do not give to get rich; we give because we
trust our generous Father.

Today, give yourself to the Lord first. Then hold everything else
with an open hand. Ask Him to make generosity a growing grace in your
life, and whatever He leads you to give, give it willingly,
thankfully, and joyfully. Your Father sees the heart—and a heart
surrendered to Jesus is something He can use for His glory.
