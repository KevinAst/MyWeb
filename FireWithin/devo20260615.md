As Paul met with the elders from Ephesus for what would likely be
their final time together, he reflected on the ministry God had
entrusted to him. Among the things he emphasized, one stood above the
rest: he had faithfully taught them the whole counsel of God. He did
not select only the comforting truths of Scripture while avoiding the
challenging ones. He taught God's Word fully because he knew that
spiritual health depends upon receiving all that God has spoken.

The Lord reveals His character throughout the entirety of
Scripture. We learn of His love, mercy, and grace, but we also learn
of His holiness, justice, and truth. When we embrace only the parts
that comfort us, we gain an incomplete understanding of who God
is. Yet when we humbly receive the whole counsel of His Word, we are
strengthened, corrected, encouraged, and transformed.

God's Word is not given merely to inform us but to shape us. There are
passages that bring deep reassurance to the weary heart. There are
also passages that expose sin, call us to repentance, and direct us
toward obedience. Both are expressions of God's love. A wise Father
not only comforts His children, He also instructs them for their good.

The Christian life is not sustained by selective obedience but by a
growing submission to all that God has revealed. As we open our Bibles
each day, we should ask the Lord not only to show us what is pleasant
to hear but also what we need to hear. The Spirit uses the truth of
Scripture to mature believers and prepare them to stand firm in a
world filled with confusion and compromise.
