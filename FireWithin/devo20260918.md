Marriage brings two different people together, and those differences
often become most apparent when there is disagreement. Scripture does
not teach us that a healthy marriage will never experience
conflict. Instead, it teaches believers how to respond to one another
when conflict comes.

Our natural tendency is to defend ourselves, rehearse past offenses,
or insist that the other person understand our perspective. But Christ
calls us to something different. Kindness, tenderness, and forgiveness
are not merely good communication techniques. They are expressions of
the grace we have already received from God.

This is especially important in marriage. A husband and wife will
sometimes hear things differently, process difficulties differently,
and need different amounts of time before they are ready to
talk. Those differences do not necessarily mean that one person is
right and the other is wrong. They remind us that marriage requires
patience, humility, prayer, and a willingness to listen.

The deepest foundation for forgiveness is not the worthiness of our
spouse. Paul points us back to Christ: we forgive because God has
forgiven us in Christ. The gospel becomes the pattern for how we treat
one another.

This does not mean ignoring sin or pretending that painful words do
not matter. Some wounds require honest conversation, repentance,
forgiveness, and time for trust to be restored. But believers do not
have to approach those difficulties in the strength of the flesh. We
can bring them before the Lord and ask Him to soften our hearts,
govern our words, and help us pursue reconciliation. This central
emphasis is reflected throughout the marriage discussion in the
provided material.

A strong marriage is not built by two flawless people, but by two
people continually submitting themselves to the lordship and grace of
Jesus Christ.
