M{ bibleLink(`dan.7@@Daniel 7`) }M reminds us that earthly kingdoms
rise and fall, but the rule of Christ is everlasting.

Daniel was given a troubling vision of powerful kingdoms, violent
rulers, and opposition against God and His people. Much of what he saw
overwhelmed him. Yet the central truth of the chapter is not the
strength of earthly powers, but the absolute sovereignty of God. Human
rulers may exercise authority for a season, but none of them can
overturn what God has appointed.

In the midst of Daniel’s vision, he sees “One like the Son of Man”
_(M{ bibleLink(`dan.7.13@@Daniel 7:13@@NKJV`) }M)_ receiving dominion,
glory, and a kingdom. Jesus later used this title, Son of Man, of
Himself _(M{ bibleLink(`mat.26.63-64@@Matthew 26:63-64`) }M)_. 
Daniel’s vision ultimately points us beyond unstable human governments
to the eternal reign of Christ.

This is where believers must keep their attention. Scripture does
reveal that evil will oppose God and that rebellion will increase
before final judgment. But biblical prophecy was never given merely to
satisfy curiosity about future events. It calls God’s people to
faithfulness, discernment, and hope.

We should therefore be careful not to become consumed with
speculation. Our confidence does not rest in correctly identifying
every symbol or predicting every development. Our confidence rests in
the certainty that Jesus Christ reigns, will judge evil, and will
establish His kingdom in its fullness.

That truth should shape how we live now. We remain faithful to Christ,
proclaim the gospel, pursue holiness, and trust God’s sovereign
purposes even when the world appears unstable. The future belongs
neither to human power nor to evil. It belongs to the King whose
kingdom cannot be destroyed.

When the future seems uncertain, remember that Christ’s throne is not
uncertain.
